Foundations • Core

Electrophysiology, conduction, and reentry

Connect action potentials and conduction pathways to bradycardia, tachycardia, and antiarrhythmic effects.

Mechanism

Automaticity initiates impulses; conduction and refractoriness determine propagation. Reentry requires two pathways with different conduction and refractory properties plus unidirectional block. Sodium-channel tissue conducts rapidly; calcium-dependent nodal tissue conducts slowly and is strongly autonomically modulated.

Three layers of understanding

Start simple, add a mental model, then prove you understand it by teaching the mechanism back without jargon.

5

Explain it like I’m five

Heart cells are tiny batteries. Ions crossing gates create a wave; specialized wires spread it. A rhythm can loop when one road blocks forward travel but another lets the wave return later.

↔

A mental model that sticks

Imagine runners on a circular track whose gates need recovery time. A one-way block plus slow travel can let a runner circle forever.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

Feynman technique

Teach it back

Teach ventricular and nodal action potentials, then derive the three requirements for reentry and predict what slowing conduction or prolonging refractoriness does.

Your explanation is complete only if it can answer:

  • 1. Contrast sodium- and calcium-dependent upstrokes.
  • 2. Explain unidirectional block.
  • 3. Connect channel effects to ECG intervals.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

Learning objectives

  1. 01Contrast nodal and working-myocyte action potentials.
  2. 02Derive automaticity, triggered activity, and reentry from cellular behavior.
  3. 03Predict ECG and proarrhythmic effects of channel blockade.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Fast and slow responses

Atrial, ventricular, and Purkinje phase 0 depends mainly on fast sodium current; SA and AV nodal phase 0 depends on calcium current. This is why sodium blockers widen QRS and AV-nodal agents prolong PR.

Refractoriness

Effective refractory period is the interval during which a propagated response cannot occur. Conduction velocity and refractoriness jointly determine whether a returning wavefront encounters excitable tissue.

Three tachycardia mechanisms

Enhanced automaticity accelerates spontaneous phase-4 depolarization; triggered activity follows afterdepolarizations; reentry requires a circuit, unidirectional block, and conduction slow enough for recovered tissue ahead.

II

Clinical reasoning

Read mechanism from behavior

Warm-up and cool-down suggest automaticity; abrupt onset and termination favor reentry; pause dependence and long QT suggest early afterdepolarizations. These are clues, not proofs.

Use the ECG as anatomy

PR reflects atrial-to-ventricular conduction, QRS ventricular activation, and QT total ventricular depolarization plus repolarization. Changes localize drug or disease effects.

Avoid substrate-blind prescribing

Structural disease, ischemia, renal/hepatic function, electrolytes, bradycardia, and interacting drugs alter efficacy and torsades or conduction risk.

Worked clinical problem

A patient taking sotalol develops bradycardia, QT prolongation, and recurrent polymorphic VT after aggressive diuresis.

1

Substrate

Bradycardia lengthens repolarization and diuresis may lower potassium and magnesium.

2

Mechanism

Delayed repolarization promotes early afterdepolarizations and pause-dependent torsades.

3

Action

Stop QT-prolonging drugs, correct electrolytes, give magnesium, and increase rate when recurrent.

Synthesis

The rhythm is not generic “VT.” Its pause dependence, long QT, and acquired substrate identify triggered activity and determine treatment.

III

Retrieval practice

01What are the prerequisites for reentry? +

A circuit, unidirectional block, and slow enough conduction for tissue ahead to recover.

02Which current drives AV-nodal phase 0? +

L-type calcium current.

03Why can sodium-channel blockade widen QRS more at fast rates? +

Use dependence: less recovery time leaves more channels blocked.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

Automaticity initiates impulses; conduction and refractoriness determine propagation. Reentry requires two pathways with different conduction and refractory properties plus unidirectional block. Sodium-channel tissue conducts rapidly; calcium-dependent nodal tissue conducts slowly and is strongly autonomically modulated.

→
2

Recognize the pattern

A narrow QRS usually means ventricular activation used the His-Purkinje system; it does not prove a supraventricular mechanism. AV dissociation, capture beats, and fusion beats strongly favor ventricular tachycardia.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Measure rate, regularity, atrial activity, AV relationship, QRS width, and axis in that order. Compare with a baseline ECG and search for reversible triggers: ischemia, electrolytes, drugs, hypoxia, and endocrine disease.

→
4

Act and reassess

Stability comes first: synchronized cardioversion for unstable tachycardia and pacing support for unstable bradycardia. Choose rhythm drugs by substrate, organ function, interactions, and proarrhythmic risk.

By the end, you should be able to

  • 02Recognize the highest-yield clinical and physiologic findings.
  • 03Choose a test because of the decision it can change.
  • 04Start appropriate management and name what must be reassessed.
