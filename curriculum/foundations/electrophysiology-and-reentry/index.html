Electrophysiology, conduction, and reentry

Connect action potentials and conduction pathways to bradycardia, tachycardia, and antiarrhythmic effects.

Mechanism

Automaticity initiates impulses; conduction and refractoriness determine propagation. Reentry requires two pathways with different conduction and refractory properties plus unidirectional block. Sodium-channel tissue conducts rapidly; calcium-dependent nodal tissue conducts slowly and is strongly autonomically modulated.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

Automaticity initiates impulses; conduction and refractoriness determine propagation. Reentry requires two pathways with different conduction and refractory properties plus unidirectional block. Sodium-channel tissue conducts rapidly; calcium-dependent nodal tissue conducts slowly and is strongly autonomically modulated.

2

Recognize the pattern

A narrow QRS usually means ventricular activation used the His-Purkinje system; it does not prove a supraventricular mechanism. AV dissociation, capture beats, and fusion beats strongly favor ventricular tachycardia.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Measure rate, regularity, atrial activity, AV relationship, QRS width, and axis in that order. Compare with a baseline ECG and search for reversible triggers: ischemia, electrolytes, drugs, hypoxia, and endocrine disease.

→
4

Act and reassess

Stability comes first: synchronized cardioversion for unstable tachycardia and pacing support for unstable bradycardia. Choose rhythm drugs by substrate, organ function, interactions, and proarrhythmic risk.

Check your understanding

  1. Mechanism: What variable changes first, and what changes downstream?
  2. Discrimination: Which finding best separates this process from its closest mimic?
  3. Testing: What result would actually change what you do next?
  4. Safety: Which common shortcut creates the greatest risk? Hint: Calling every regular narrow-complex tachycardia “SVT” without defining the mechanism.

Findings

  • A narrow QRS usually means ventricular activation used the His-Purkinje system; it does not prove a supraventricular mechanism.
  • AV dissociation, capture beats, and fusion beats strongly favor ventricular tachycardia.
02

Tests and interpretation

  • Measure rate, regularity, atrial activity, AV relationship, QRS width, and axis in that order.
  • Compare with a baseline ECG and search for reversible triggers: ischemia, electrolytes, drugs, hypoxia, and endocrine disease.
03

Management

  • Stability comes first: synchronized cardioversion for unstable tachycardia and pacing support for unstable bradycardia.
  • Choose rhythm drugs by substrate, organ function, interactions, and proarrhythmic risk.
04

Common pitfalls

  • Calling every regular narrow-complex tachycardia “SVT” without defining the mechanism.
  • Using AV-nodal blockade in an irregular wide-complex rhythm when pre-excited AF is possible.
