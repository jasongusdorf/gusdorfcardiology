The cardiac cycle and pressure-volume loops

Use pressure, volume, and valve state to reason through preload, afterload, contractility, and compliance.

Mechanism

A pressure-volume loop links ventricular filling, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, and isovolumetric relaxation. Preload changes end-diastolic volume; afterload changes the pressure the ventricle must overcome; contractility shifts the end-systolic pressure-volume relation; impaired compliance raises diastolic pressure at any given volume.

Build intuition first

Three layers of understanding

Start simple, add a mental model, then prove you understand it by teaching the mechanism back without jargon.

5

Explain it like I’m five

A ventricle fills, squeezes with both valves shut, ejects, and relaxes with both valves shut. A pressure–volume loop is simply a picture of those four moments.

↔

A mental model that sticks

A syringe has inlet and outlet check valves: preload is starting fill, afterload is resistance to the plunger, contractility is hand strength, and compliance is wall stiffness.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

Feynman technique

Teach it back

Draw a blank loop and label each valve event. Redraw it after increased preload, afterload, contractility, and stiffness.

Your explanation is complete only if it can answer:

  • 1. Separate pressure from volume.
  • 2. Explain why EF is not contractility.
  • 3. Derive stroke volume from EDV and ESV.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

A pressure-volume loop links ventricular filling, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, and isovolumetric relaxation. Preload changes end-diastolic volume; afterload changes the pressure the ventricle must overcome; contractility shifts the end-systolic pressure-volume relation; impaired compliance raises diastolic pressure at any given volume.

2

Recognize the pattern

S3 points toward rapid filling into a volume-loaded ventricle; S4 reflects atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle. A narrow pulse pressure can signal low stroke volume; a wide pulse pressure suggests rapid runoff or reduced arterial compliance.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Read filling pressures alongside output: congestion and perfusion are separate axes. Use Doppler and tissue velocity as estimates of flow and relaxation, not direct measurements of left atrial pressure in every context.

→
4

Act and reassess

Match therapy to physiology: remove excess volume, reduce impedance, improve contractility only when needed, and correct the lesion driving the loop. Reassess after an intervention because the same blood pressure can conceal a different stroke volume.

Equations that sharpen the bedside model

Stroke volume

SV=EDV−ESV

The horizontal width of the pressure-volume loop.

Units/terms: Volumes in mL

Ejection fraction

EF=EDVEDV−ESV​×100%

A ratio of chamber emptying—not a direct measure of contractility.

Mean arterial pressure

MAP≈DBP+31​(SBP−DBP)

A useful approximation at normal heart rates; invasive integration is more exact.
