The cardiac cycle and pressure-volume loops

Use pressure, volume, and valve state to reason through preload, afterload, contractility, and compliance.

Mechanism

A pressure-volume loop links ventricular filling, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, and isovolumetric relaxation. Preload changes end-diastolic volume; afterload changes the pressure the ventricle must overcome; contractility shifts the end-systolic pressure-volume relation; impaired compliance raises diastolic pressure at any given volume.

01

Findings

  • S3 points toward rapid filling into a volume-loaded ventricle; S4 reflects atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle.
  • A narrow pulse pressure can signal low stroke volume; a wide pulse pressure suggests rapid runoff or reduced arterial compliance.
02

Tests and interpretation

  • Read filling pressures alongside output: congestion and perfusion are separate axes.
  • Use Doppler and tissue velocity as estimates of flow and relaxation, not direct measurements of left atrial pressure in every context.
03

Management

  • Match therapy to physiology: remove excess volume, reduce impedance, improve contractility only when needed, and correct the lesion driving the loop.
  • Reassess after an intervention because the same blood pressure can conceal a different stroke volume.
04

Common pitfalls

  • Treating ejection fraction as contractility.
  • Assuming a normal EF excludes heart failure or low forward flow.
