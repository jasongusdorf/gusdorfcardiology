The cardiac cycle and pressure-volume loops

Use pressure, volume, and valve state to reason through preload, afterload, contractility, and compliance.

Mechanism

A pressure-volume loop links ventricular filling, isovolumetric contraction, ejection, and isovolumetric relaxation. Preload changes end-diastolic volume; afterload changes the pressure the ventricle must overcome; contractility shifts the end-systolic pressure-volume relation; impaired compliance raises diastolic pressure at any given volume.

Explain it like I’m five

A ventricle fills, squeezes with both valves shut, ejects, and relaxes with both valves shut. A pressure–volume loop is simply a picture of those four moments.

↔

A mental model that sticks

A syringe has inlet and outlet check valves: preload is starting fill, afterload is resistance to the plunger, contractility is hand strength, and compliance is wall stiffness.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

Feynman technique

Teach it back

Draw a blank loop and label each valve event. Redraw it after increased preload, afterload, contractility, and stiffness.

Your explanation is complete only if it can answer:

  • 2. Explain why EF is not contractility.
  • 3. Derive stroke volume from EDV and ESV.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

Learning objectives

  1. 01Label every phase, valve event, and heart sound on a pressure–volume loop.
  2. 02Predict loop changes with preload, afterload, contractility, compliance, and valve lesions.
  3. 03Separate ejection fraction, stroke volume, contractility, and filling pressure.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Valve-state logic

Filling occurs with mitral valve open and aortic valve closed; isovolumetric contraction and relaxation occur with both closed; ejection occurs with aortic open and mitral closed. Valve events follow pressure crossover, not a timer.

End-systolic relation

The end-systolic pressure–volume relation approximates load-independent contractile state: greater contractility steepens and shifts it left. The arterial elastance line summarizes effective afterload.

Diastolic relation

The end-diastolic pressure–volume relation is nonlinear. Reduced compliance shifts it upward: a small volume increment can cause a large filling-pressure rise without reduced EF.

II

Clinical reasoning

Move one variable first

Increased preload moves end-diastolic volume right and widens the loop. Increased afterload raises systolic pressure and end-systolic volume, narrowing stroke volume initially. Increased contractility lowers end-systolic volume.

Valve lesions erase phases

Regurgitation abolishes true isovolumetric phases because volume changes while both normal valves should be closed. Stenosis creates a systolic or diastolic gradient across an open valve.

Integrate bedside data

Blood pressure, pulse pressure, JVP, Doppler, and output describe different parts of the loop. A normal MAP does not establish adequate stroke volume if SVR is high.

Worked clinical problem

After phenylephrine, an anesthetized patient has higher blood pressure, lower stroke volume, and unchanged intrinsic contractility.

1

Perturbation

Phenylephrine increases arterial resistance and effective afterload.

2

Loop response

The ventricle must reach a higher pressure before ejection and ends systole at a larger volume.

3

Observed consequence

Loop height rises while width and stroke volume fall; EF may fall without primary myocardial injury.

Synthesis

Name the loading change before interpreting the EF. A pressure–volume loop prevents the common error of equating an afterload-mediated EF change with altered contractility.

III

Retrieval practice

01Which loop dimension is stroke volume? +

Its horizontal width, EDV minus ESV.

02What happens to ESV when afterload acutely rises? +

It rises.

03Why can HFpEF cause pulmonary edema with normal EF? +

A stiff ventricle reaches high diastolic pressure at relatively normal volumes while fractional emptying remains preserved.

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

Recognize the pattern

S3 points toward rapid filling into a volume-loaded ventricle; S4 reflects atrial contraction into a stiff ventricle. A narrow pulse pressure can signal low stroke volume; a wide pulse pressure suggests rapid runoff or reduced arterial compliance.

3

Confirm and classify

Read filling pressures alongside output: congestion and perfusion are separate axes. Use Doppler and tissue velocity as estimates of flow and relaxation, not direct measurements of left atrial pressure in every context.

→
4

Act and reassess

Match therapy to physiology: remove excess volume, reduce impedance, improve contractility only when needed, and correct the lesion driving the loop. Reassess after an intervention because the same blood pressure can conceal a different stroke volume.
