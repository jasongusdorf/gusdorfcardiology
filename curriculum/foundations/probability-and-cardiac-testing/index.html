Probability before testing

Choose tests by the question they can answer and the decision threshold they can cross.

Mechanism

A test result changes probability according to its likelihood ratio and the pretest probability. Testing helps most in the intermediate zone: low probability invites false positives, while very high probability may justify treatment or definitive testing without an intermediate screen.

Explain it like I’m five

A test does not announce truth; it updates a starting guess. The same positive result means more when disease was already plausible.

A mental model that sticks

A smoke alarm means something different in a kitchen and a fireworks factory because the starting chance of fire differs.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

  • 3. Know when more testing cannot help.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

Learning objectives

  1. 01Convert probability to odds and apply likelihood ratios.
  2. 02Choose testing thresholds from consequences and available actions.
  3. 03Recognize verification, spectrum, incorporation, and referral biases.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Probability is contextual

Sensitivity and specificity describe test behavior in studied populations; predictive values depend on prevalence. Likelihood ratios connect test performance to an individual pretest estimate.

Threshold model

Below the test threshold, disease is too unlikely to justify testing; above the treatment threshold, action may be appropriate without another screening test. Testing has maximum value between them.

Calibration before precision

A numerical score is useful only if its population, endpoint, and calibration fit the patient. Apparent precision cannot rescue a poor target diagnosis or biased estimate.

II

Clinical reasoning

Define the decision

Write the diagnosis, result categories, and action each category changes before ordering. “More information” is not itself a clinical decision.

Treat indeterminate honestly

Image quality, gray-zone biomarkers, and discordant studies should update uncertainty rather than being forced into positive/negative bins.

Consider harms

Radiation, contrast, bleeding, incidental findings, downstream procedures, false reassurance, and delay all belong in the expected-value calculation.

Worked clinical problem

A low-risk patient with atypical pain has a mildly abnormal screening stress test and is referred for angiography.

1

Pretest

Low disease prevalence makes false positives comparatively common.

2

Update

Apply the test’s actual likelihood ratio rather than treating “positive” as disease.

3

Decision

Reassess image quality and whether an anatomic or alternative test can resolve uncertainty without immediate invasive risk.

Synthesis

The error began before the stress test: no explicit pretest probability, target lesion, or management threshold was defined.

III

Retrieval practice

01What does LR+ equal? +

Sensitivity divided by one minus specificity.

02When is testing usually most valuable? +

At intermediate probability, between test and treatment thresholds.

03Does a 90% sensitive test imply a 90% chance of disease when positive? +

No; posttest probability also depends on specificity and pretest probability.

Equations that sharpen the bedside model

Odds form of Bayes

Posttest odds=Pretest odds×LR

A likelihood ratio updates odds; it does not replace the pretest estimate.

Worked example

Given: Pretest probability 20%; LR+ 5

Substitute: Pretest odds 0.20/0.80 = 0.25; posttest odds 0.25 × 5 = 1.25

Result: Posttest probability 1.25/2.25 ≈ 56%

Decision: A meaningful positive result does not automatically cross every treatment threshold.

Probability to odds

Odds=1−pp​

Convert probability to odds before applying a likelihood ratio.

Worked example

Given: Pretest probability 30%

Substitute: 0.30 / (1 − 0.30)

Result: Odds 0.429

Decision: Multiply odds by a likelihood ratio, then convert back to probability.
