Probability before testing

Choose tests by the question they can answer and the decision threshold they can cross.

Mechanism

A test result changes probability according to its likelihood ratio and the pretest probability. Testing helps most in the intermediate zone: low probability invites false positives, while very high probability may justify treatment or definitive testing without an intermediate screen.

01

Findings

  • Build the pretest estimate from symptoms, tempo, risk, examination, ECG, and prior studies.
  • Separate “Is disease present?” from “Is it causing this presentation?” and “Will defining it change management?”
02

Tests and interpretation

  • State the target diagnosis and the action threshold before ordering.
  • Use serial measurements when trajectory carries more information than a single value.
03

Management

  • Stop when additional testing cannot plausibly change treatment.
  • Escalate when a dangerous diagnosis remains above the acceptable miss threshold.
04

Common pitfalls

  • Treating a positive stress test as synonymous with obstructive coronary disease.
  • Ordering broad panels that generate incidental abnormalities without a plan.
