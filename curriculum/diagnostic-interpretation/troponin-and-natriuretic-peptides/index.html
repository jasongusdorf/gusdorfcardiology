Diagnostic interpretation • Core

Troponin and natriuretic peptides

Distinguish myocardial injury from infarction and wall stress from a heart-failure diagnosis.

Mechanism

Troponin indicates myocardial injury; myocardial infarction additionally requires evidence of ischemia. BNP and NT-proBNP rise with myocardial wall stress but are modified by age, rhythm, renal function, body habitus, and treatment.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

  • •Classify myocardial injury by acuity and mechanism.
  • •Apply assay-specific serial pathways.
  • •Interpret natriuretic peptides using major modifiers.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Injury versus infarction

Troponin above the 99th percentile defines myocardial injury. Acute rise/fall defines acute injury. Myocardial infarction additionally requires evidence of ischemia; type 1 and type 2 MI have different mechanisms.

Delta is assay-specific

Absolute versus relative change, sampling interval, analytical variation, and baseline elevation matter. Chronic kidney disease often raises baseline troponin but does not make dynamic changes meaningless.

Natriuretic context

Age, AF, renal dysfunction, and RV strain can raise BNP/NT-proBNP; obesity and treatment can lower it. Values support or oppose a syndrome but do not replace congestion assessment.

II

Clinical reasoning

Ask why myocardium is injured

Plaque rupture, supply–demand imbalance, myocarditis, tachyarrhythmia, embolism, shock, and heart failure require different treatment.

Use serial data as a trajectory

Interpret symptom timing, ECG, magnitude and direction of change, imaging, and competing stressors together.

Do not treat the number

Antithrombotic and invasive strategies follow the ischemic mechanism and risk, not troponin positivity alone.

Worked clinical problem

A septic patient without chest pain has tachycardia, diffuse ST depression, and a modest dynamic troponin rise.

1

Classification

This is acute myocardial injury with possible ischemia from supply–demand mismatch.

2

Mechanism search

Assess hypotension, hypoxemia, anemia, tachycardia, and preexisting coronary disease.

3

Management

Treat sepsis and the mismatch; reserve type-1-MI therapy for evidence supporting acute coronary thrombosis.

Synthesis

“NSTEMI” is not a synonym for every troponin rise. Classification must preserve both injury acuity and causal mechanism.

III

Retrieval practice

01What is required beyond troponin for MI? +

Clinical evidence of myocardial ischemia.

02Does CKD make troponin uninterpretable? +

No; use baseline context, serial change, symptoms, ECG, and imaging.

03Name two factors that lower natriuretic peptides. +

Obesity and effective treatment; very early flash edema may also precede a large rise.

01

Findings

  • Classify troponin as acute versus chronic from change over time, then ischemic versus nonischemic from the full presentation.
  • Use a low natriuretic peptide to argue against HF in the right setting; a high value needs explanation rather than automatic labeling.
02

Tests and interpretation

  • Apply the assay-specific 99th percentile and validated serial algorithm.
  • Interpret deltas in relation to sampling interval and baseline concentration.
03

Management

  • Treat the mechanism: plaque rupture, supply-demand imbalance, myocarditis, PE, tachyarrhythmia, shock, or another cause.
  • Do not expose nonischemic injury to routine DAPT and anticoagulation without an indication.
04

Common pitfalls

  • Calling every elevated troponin NSTEMI.
  • Using BNP to distinguish cardiac from pulmonary dyspnea without considering overlap and comorbidity.
