Invasive hemodynamics and waveforms

Read pressures, saturations, and derived flow as one internally consistent story.

Mechanism

Catheterization samples pressure and oxygen content at defined locations. Cardiac output is measured or estimated; resistance is a pressure gradient divided by flow. Waveform timing and morphology often reveal the lesion before a derived number does.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

Learning objectives

  1. 01Recognize RA, RV, PA, and wedge waveforms.
  2. 02Validate transducer and respiratory technique.
  3. 03Derive output, shunt, resistance, and shock phenotype.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Measurement before calculation

Level at the phlebostatic axis, zero to atmosphere, inspect waveform quality, record rhythm and ventilation, and measure at an appropriate respiratory phase. A calculated PVR cannot repair a false wedge.

Waveform anatomy

RA pressure has a/c/v and x/y descents; RV has steep systolic upstroke and low diastolic pressure; PA retains diastolic pressure and a dicrotic notch; wedge reflects left-atrial pressure when truly occlusive.

Flow and resistance

Fick output divides oxygen consumption by arteriovenous oxygen-content difference. Resistance equals pressure gradient divided by flow. Step-ups in saturation localize left-to-right shunt.

II

Clinical reasoning

Demand internal consistency

Wedge morphology, oxygen saturation, fluoroscopic position, PA diastolic pressure, and clinical anatomy should agree. Large V waves, PEEP, mitral disease, and lung-zone position complicate interpretation.

Phenotype rather than threshold

Cardiac index, filling pressures, SVR, PVR, RV indices, and clinical perfusion together describe shock or pulmonary hypertension.

Measure after interventions

Vasodilator, fluid, diuretic, inotrope, or support-device response can reveal reserve and mechanism if measurement quality is preserved.

Worked clinical problem

A catheter report shows mPAP 35, wedge 28, CO 3.5 L/min, but the wedge tracing resembles PA pressure and saturation is 70%.

1

Quality concern

The “wedge” may be incomplete occlusion because its waveform and saturation are implausible for pulmonary venous blood.

2

Consequence

Using 28 would falsely classify pulmonary hypertension and distort PVR.

3

Action

Reposition, confirm occlusion waveform and saturation, and repeat before calculation or treatment.

Synthesis

Hemodynamics begins with waveforms, not the spreadsheet. Classification based on an unvalidated wedge can send therapy in the wrong direction.

III

Retrieval practice

01What is PVR? +

(Mean PA pressure minus PA wedge pressure) divided by cardiac output.

02What suggests a true wedge? +

Appropriate atrial waveform, occlusive position, and highly oxygenated wedge sample consistent with pulmonary venous blood.

03Where is the transducer leveled? +

At the right atrial level, commonly the mid-axillary phlebostatic axis.

Equations that sharpen the bedside model

Fick cardiac output

CO=CaO2​−CvO2​V˙O2​​

Flow equals oxygen consumption divided by the arteriovenous oxygen-content difference.

Worked example

Given: VO₂ 250 mL/min; arterial–venous O₂ content difference 50 mL/L

Substitute: 250 / 50

Result: 5.0 L/min

Decision: Assumed oxygen consumption can introduce major error; inspect measured saturations and sampling quality.

Systemic vascular resistance

SVR=80COMAP−RAP​

Resistance is the systemic pressure gradient divided by flow.

Units/terms: dyn·s·cm⁻⁵

Worked example

Given: MAP 70; RAP 10 mmHg; CO 3 L/min

Substitute: 80 × (70 − 10) / 3

Result: 1,600 dyn·s·cm⁻⁵

Decision: High SVR may preserve pressure while worsening forward flow in cardiogenic shock.

Pulmonary vascular resistance

PVR=COmPAP−PAWP​

Always inspect the component measurements; an implausible wedge or output makes an implausible PVR.

Units/terms: Wood units

Worked example

Given: mPAP 35; wedge 15 mmHg; CO 4 L/min

Substitute: (35 − 15) / 4

Result: 5 Wood units

Decision: Validate wedge and output before classifying pulmonary hypertension.
