Diagnostic interpretation • Advanced

Invasive hemodynamics and waveforms

Read pressures, saturations, and derived flow as one internally consistent story.

Mechanism

Catheterization samples pressure and oxygen content at defined locations. Cardiac output is measured or estimated; resistance is a pressure gradient divided by flow. Waveform timing and morphology often reveal the lesion before a derived number does.

Build intuition first

Three layers of understanding

Start simple, add a mental model, then prove you understand it by teaching the mechanism back without jargon.

5

Explain it like I’m five

Catheters place gauges inside circulation. Each waveform reports local filling, contraction, and valve behavior; calculated numbers are only as sound as raw measurements.

↔

A mental model that sticks

Troubleshoot plumbing by placing pressure gauges in every room.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

Feynman technique

Teach it back

Walk RA→RV→PA→wedge, describe waveforms and saturations, then derive flow and resistance.

Your explanation is complete only if it can answer:

  • 1. Check zero, level, rhythm, respiration.
  • 2. Resistance equals gradient divided by flow.
  • 3. Demand internal consistency.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

Catheterization samples pressure and oxygen content at defined locations. Cardiac output is measured or estimated; resistance is a pressure gradient divided by flow. Waveform timing and morphology often reveal the lesion before a derived number does.

→
2

Recognize the pattern

Confirm transducer leveling, zeroing, respiratory phase, rhythm, and waveform fidelity. Recognize ventricularization, equalization of diastolic pressures, prominent wedge v waves, discordant ventricular interaction, and pressure gradients.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Check whether Fick assumptions are plausible and whether thermodilution is distorted by severe TR or shunt. Perform saturation runs when a shunt is suspected.

→
4

Act and reassess

Use hemodynamics when uncertainty is consequential: shock phenotype, constriction versus restriction, pulmonary hypertension classification, or valve discordance.

Check your understanding

  1. Mechanism: What variable changes first, and what changes downstream?
  2. Discrimination: Which finding best separates this process from its closest mimic?
  3. Testing: What result would actually change what you do next?
  4. Safety: Which common shortcut creates the greatest risk? Hint: Treating calculated values as exact despite poor source measurements.

Equations that sharpen the bedside model

Fick cardiac output

CO=CaO2​−CvO2​V˙O2​​

Flow equals oxygen consumption divided by the arteriovenous oxygen-content difference.

Systemic vascular resistance

SVR=80COMAP−RAP​

Resistance is the systemic pressure gradient divided by flow.

Units/terms: dyn·s·cm⁻⁵

Pulmonary vascular resistance

PVR=COmPAP−PAWP​

Always inspect the component measurements; an implausible wedge or output makes an implausible PVR.

Units/terms: Wood units
