Diagnostic interpretation • Core

Systematic ECG interpretation

A reproducible read from calibration through rhythm, intervals, axis, morphology, ischemia, and comparison.

Mechanism

The ECG is a projection of depolarization and repolarization vectors over time. Every deflection depends on direction, mass, sequence, and lead orientation.

Build intuition first

Three layers of understanding

Start simple, add a mental model, then prove you understand it by teaching the mechanism back without jargon.

5

Explain it like I’m five

An ECG is twelve camera angles watching one electrical wave. A fixed checklist prevents one exciting abnormality from hiding a basic clue.

↔

A mental model that sticks

Reconstruct a crash from twelve security cameras; no single angle tells the whole story.

The analogy is a scaffold—not a substitute for the physiology.

Feynman technique

Teach it back

Read aloud from calibration through rhythm, intervals, axis, QRS, and ST–T changes; finish with synthesis and comparison.

Your explanation is complete only if it can answer:

  • 1. Verify calibration and leads.
  • 2. Separate observation from diagnosis.
  • 3. Use serial ECGs for dynamic disease.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

The ECG is a projection of depolarization and repolarization vectors over time. Every deflection depends on direction, mass, sequence, and lead orientation.

→
2

Recognize the pattern

Confirm name, time, indication, calibration, rate, rhythm, P-QRS relationship, intervals, axis, chamber patterns, QRS morphology, and ST-T changes. End with a synthesis that separates definite findings from possibilities and names the comparison.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Repeat misplaced leads before escalating a bizarre but physiologically implausible tracing. Use serial ECGs during dynamic symptoms; a single normal ECG does not exclude ACS.

→
4

Act and reassess

Act immediately on unstable rhythms and diagnostic occlusion patterns while obtaining confirmatory data in parallel.

By the end, you should be able to

  • 01Explain the underlying mechanism without relying on a memorized label.
  • 02Recognize the highest-yield clinical and physiologic findings.
  • 03Choose a test because of the decision it can change.
  • 04Start appropriate management and name what must be reassessed.

Check your understanding

  1. Mechanism: What variable changes first, and what changes downstream?
  2. Discrimination: Which finding best separates this process from its closest mimic?
  3. Testing: What result would actually change what you do next?
  4. Safety: Which common shortcut creates the greatest risk? Hint: Letting the automated interpretation anchor the read.

04

Common pitfalls

  • Letting the automated interpretation anchor the read.
  • Calling every ST elevation STEMI without checking morphology, reciprocal change, symptoms, and prior ECGs.
