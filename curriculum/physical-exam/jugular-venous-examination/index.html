Physical exam • Core

Jugular venous examination

Estimate right atrial pressure and read the venous waveform as a right-heart tracing.

Mechanism

The JVP is a visible manometer connected to the right atrium. The a wave is atrial contraction, c reflects tricuspid closure/bulging, x descent is atrial relaxation and downward annular motion, v is venous filling, and y descent is early ventricular filling.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

The JVP is a visible manometer connected to the right atrium. The a wave is atrial contraction, c reflects tricuspid closure/bulging, x descent is atrial relaxation and downward annular motion, v is venous filling, and y descent is early ventricular filling.

→
2

Recognize the pattern

Large a waves suggest resistance to right atrial emptying; cannon a waves reflect AV dissociation. Prominent v waves point toward tricuspid regurgitation. Kussmaul sign suggests impaired RV filling, including constriction, restriction, or RV infarction.

→
3

Confirm and classify

Identify the biphasic venous waveform, change the head angle, and measure vertical height above the sternal angle. Use hepatojugular reflux as a sustained preload challenge, not a brief abdominal jab.

→
4

Act and reassess

Trend JVP with symptoms, weight, renal function, and perfusion during decongestion.
