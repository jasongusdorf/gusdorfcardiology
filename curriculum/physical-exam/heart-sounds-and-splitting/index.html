Physical exam • Core

Heart sounds and splitting

Translate S1–S4, splitting, clicks, snaps, and rubs into valve motion and filling physiology.

Mechanism

S1 and S2 mark closure of the AV and semilunar valves. Respiratory splitting of S2 follows delayed pulmonic closure during inspiration. Extra sounds occur when flow abruptly decelerates, a valve reaches its opening limit, or inflamed pericardial surfaces move against one another.

01

Findings

  • Fixed S2 splitting suggests an atrial-level shunt; paradoxical splitting reflects delayed LV emptying.
  • An opening snap after S2 suggests a mobile stenotic mitral valve; a systolic click that moves with loading conditions suggests mitral valve prolapse.
02

Tests and interpretation

  • Listen at multiple sites with diaphragm and bell, then repeat in left lateral and upright positions.
  • Time sounds against the carotid pulse when systole is uncertain.
03

Management

  • Use the sound to choose confirmatory imaging rather than treating auscultation as a final severity grade.
04

Common pitfalls

  • Calling any low-frequency diastolic sound an S3.
  • Missing a rub because it is transient or has only one audible component.
