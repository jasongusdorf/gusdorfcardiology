Disease processes • Core

Pericardial and myocardial inflammation

Distinguish pericarditis, myocarditis, myopericarditis, effusion, tamponade, and constriction.

Mechanism

Pericardial inflammation produces pain, friction, fluid, and sometimes constrictive scarring. Myocardial inflammation causes injury, edema, systolic dysfunction, arrhythmia, and occasionally fulminant shock; overlap syndromes are common.

Masterclass

From structure to bedside decisions

This is the working layer: build the mechanism, discriminate among competing explanations, and use the result to make a decision.

Learning objectives

  1. 01Distinguish pericarditis, myocarditis, myopericarditis, infarction, and tamponade.
  2. 02Use CMR and biopsy for the questions they answer.
  3. 03Manage inflammation while protecting ventricular and rhythm outcomes.
I

Anatomy and physiology

Different compartments

Pericarditis affects the serosal sac and produces positional pleuritic pain, rub, effusion, or constriction. Myocarditis injures myocardium and can cause HF, arrhythmia, block, shock, or infarct-like presentation.

Tamponade physiology

Pericardial pressure restricts filling; consequence depends on accumulation rate, volume, chamber pressures, and ventricular interdependence. A small rapid effusion can be more dangerous than a large chronic one.

Diagnostic tools

CMR detects edema and nonischemic injury patterns. Endomyocardial biopsy is reserved for scenarios where histology changes urgent therapy, such as fulminant unexplained HF with malignant arrhythmia or block.

II

Clinical reasoning

Risk-stratify early

Fever, subacute course, large effusion, tamponade, immunosuppression, trauma, anticoagulation, myocardial involvement, arrhythmia, or HF should prompt admission or intensive evaluation.

Treat the compartment

Uncomplicated pericarditis generally receives anti-inflammatory therapy plus colchicine; myocarditis management focuses on HF/rhythm support and cause-specific therapy only when established.

Prescribe recovery

Restrict strenuous exercise after myocarditis and reassess symptoms, ventricular function, biomarkers, and rhythm before return.

Worked clinical problem

A young adult has chest pain, diffuse ST elevation, troponin elevation, ventricular ectopy, and mildly reduced EF.

1

Compartment

Troponin, ectopy, and dysfunction establish myocardial involvement rather than isolated pericarditis.

2

Testing

CMR can characterize nonischemic inflammation; exclude ACS when the presentation warrants.

3

Plan

Monitor rhythm and function, treat HF physiology, avoid exercise, and arrange objective recovery assessment.

Synthesis

The label myopericarditis changes risk, monitoring, and return-to-activity decisions even when chest pain resembles uncomplicated pericarditis.

III

Retrieval practice

01Does effusion size alone diagnose tamponade? +

No; tamponade is hemodynamic compromise.

02When does biopsy matter most? +

When a specific histologic diagnosis would change urgent treatment in severe or unusual myocarditis presentations.

03Why restrict exercise in myocarditis? +

Adrenergic stress may increase arrhythmia and injury risk during active inflammation.

Deep dive

A reusable reasoning framework

Move from physiology to pattern recognition, diagnostic proof, and an action that changes the patient’s trajectory.

1

Build the mechanism

Pericardial inflammation produces pain, friction, fluid, and sometimes constrictive scarring. Myocardial inflammation causes injury, edema, systolic dysfunction, arrhythmia, and occasionally fulminant shock; overlap syndromes are common.

→
2

Recognize the pattern

Pericarditis is a clinical diagnosis supported by characteristic pain, rub, widespread ST/PR changes, and new or worsening effusion. Myocarditis spans chest pain with injury, new HF, conduction disease, ventricular arrhythmia, and shock.

→
3

Confirm and classify

ECG, troponin, inflammatory markers, echo, and CMR form the usual noninvasive core. Endomyocardial biopsy is targeted to presentations where histology can change urgent management.

→
4

Act and reassess

Treat uncomplicated pericarditis with anti-inflammatory therapy plus colchicine when appropriate and restrict strenuous activity. Manage myocarditis by phenotype, avoid exercise during active disease, and escalate rapidly for shock or malignant arrhythmia.
