Aortic dissection

Title: 30 Aortic dissection Case Presentation: Description: PLAX: dilated aortic root and ascending aorta

From Wikimedia Commons · Openly licensed teaching media

Reading order mnemonic: VIEWS

View & quality → Identify chambers → Evaluate function → Watch valves/walls → Seek measurements & missing data.

First name the acoustic window and view, then confirm orientation and image quality before interpreting anatomy. Sweep through the loop more than once: chambers and valves first, global function second, then the focal abnormality.

Interpret

Source label vs. educational read

What the original source states

Title: 30 Aortic dissection Case Presentation: Description: PLAX: dilated aortic root and ascending aorta

What in the image would support that label

  • Look for an intimal flap separating true and false lumens, aortic regurgitation, pericardial effusion, and branch-vessel involvement when the field of view permits.

Apply it clinically

A demonstrated flap is a high-risk structural finding requiring complete aortic imaging and urgent clinical integration.

Keep the differential open

What else could look like this?

  • •True intimal flap
  • •Reverberation or motion artifact
  • •Atherosclerotic plaque or intramural hematoma

Complete the study

What data are still missing?

  • •Complete gated CT/MR or TEE evaluation of the aorta
  • •Aortic regurgitation, branch vessels, and pericardium
  • •Clinical syndrome and pulse/perfusion findings

Read it yourself

What feature makes an apparent aortic flap more convincing?

Reveal teaching answer →
Teaching answer: Independent motion with separation of true and false lumens in more than one plane, ideally confirmed by definitive aortic imaging.

Interpretive pitfalls

  • Motion artifact in the ascending aorta can mimic a flap.
