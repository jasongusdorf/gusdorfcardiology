Current cardiology guidelines

ACC/AHA and ESC clinical practice guidelines published from 2021 onward. When a newer edition updates the same topic, only the newest edition is retained.

Last reviewed September 17, 2026 · 37 documents · Official publisher links

ACC/AHA

15 current documents
2026•Pulmonary vascular disease

Acute pulmonary embolism

2026 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Acute Pulmonary Embolism

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Risk-stratify immediately by hemodynamic status, right-ventricular injury, biomarkers, and clinical risk rather than clot burden alone.
  • Anticoagulate promptly when safe; reserve systemic thrombolysis, catheter therapy, or surgery for shock or selected patients deteriorating despite anticoagulation.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.005

2026•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome

2026 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Stage CKM risk across adiposity, diabetes, kidney disease, and subclinical or clinical cardiovascular disease rather than treating each in isolation.
  • Prioritize weight, blood pressure, lipids, glycemia, kidney protection, and therapies with proven cardiovascular benefit through coordinated longitudinal care.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2026.03.056

2026•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Dyslipidemia

2026 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Management of Dyslipidemia

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Match LDL-C lowering intensity to absolute ASCVD risk; use high-intensity statin therapy first when indicated and verify adherence and response.
  • Add ezetimibe, a PCSK9-directed therapy, or other nonstatin treatment when LDL-C remains above the risk-based threshold or statins are not tolerated.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.016

2025•Coronary and ischemic disease

Acute coronary syndromes

2025 ACC/AHA/ACEP/NAEMSP/SCAI Guideline for the Management of Patients With Acute Coronary Syndromes

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use symptoms, serial ECGs, high-sensitivity troponin, instability, and ischemic risk together; do not delay reperfusion for a diagnostic occlusion pattern.
  • Pair an invasive/revascularization strategy with individualized antithrombotic duration, intensive lipid lowering, cardiac rehabilitation, and secondary prevention.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2024.11.009

2025•Structural and congenital disease

Adult congenital heart disease

2025 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Management of Adults With Congenital Heart Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use lesion-specific surveillance and adult congenital expertise because repaired anatomy does not eliminate late ventricular, valvular, rhythm, aortic, or pulmonary vascular complications.
  • Integrate exercise, pregnancy, endocarditis prevention, arrhythmia, and reintervention decisions into lifelong care.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.09.006

2025•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

High blood pressure

2025 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Prevention, Detection, Evaluation, and Management of High Blood Pressure in Adults

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Confirm persistent hypertension with accurate office technique and out-of-office measurement when feasible; assess global cardiovascular and kidney risk.
  • Combine lifestyle treatment with timely medication, generally favoring proven first-line classes and combination therapy when substantially above goal.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.05.007

2024•Heart failure and cardiomyopathy

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy

2024 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Define obstruction, symptoms, arrhythmia, fibrosis, family history, and sudden-death risk; screen first-degree relatives with imaging and/or genetics.
  • Use shared decisions for medical therapy, septal reduction, ICD implantation, exercise, and pregnancy rather than a single wall-thickness threshold.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2024.02.014

2024•Aortic and peripheral vascular disease

Lower-extremity peripheral artery disease

2024 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Management of Lower Extremity Peripheral Artery Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use the ankle–brachial index to establish PAD and recognize chronic symptomatic disease, chronic limb-threatening ischemia, and acute limb ischemia as distinct syndromes.
  • Provide antiplatelet/antithrombotic therapy when indicated, high-intensity statin treatment, smoking cessation, structured exercise, foot care, and revascularization for limb threat or refractory limitation.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2024.02.013

2024•Perioperative care

Perioperative cardiovascular management

2024 AHA/ACC Guideline for Perioperative Cardiovascular Management for Noncardiac Surgery

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Order testing only when it will change management; combine surgical risk, functional capacity, clinical risk, and unstable cardiac conditions.
  • Continue most chronic cardiovascular therapy, manage antithrombotics deliberately, and reserve prophylactic coronary revascularization for indications that would exist without surgery.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2024.06.013

2023•Arrhythmias and devices

Atrial fibrillation

2023 ACC/AHA/ACCP/HRS Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Prevent stroke according to validated thromboembolic risk and prefer a DOAC for most eligible patients; bleeding scores should identify modifiable risk, not automatically deny anticoagulation.
  • Treat symptoms with deliberate rate or rhythm control, consider earlier ablation, and modify the substrate—weight, activity, blood pressure, sleep apnea, alcohol, and diabetes.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2023.08.017

2023•Coronary and ischemic disease

Chronic coronary disease

2023 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Management of Patients With Chronic Coronary Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use lifestyle treatment, high-intensity lipid lowering, and disease-modifying therapy to reduce events; antianginal treatment should be individualized to symptoms and comorbidity.
  • Routine revascularization does not improve survival for every stable patient; use it for refractory symptoms or anatomy in which prognostic benefit is established.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2023.04.003

2022•Aortic and peripheral vascular disease

Aortic disease

2022 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Aortic Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use consistent measurement technique and modality to follow the aorta; growth rate, diameter, genetic syndrome, family history, and body size all affect intervention thresholds.
  • Control blood pressure, counsel on exercise and pregnancy, screen relatives when heritable disease is possible, and treat acute aortic syndromes as time-critical emergencies.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2022.08.004

2022•Heart failure and cardiomyopathy

Heart failure

2022 AHA/ACC/HFSA Guideline for the Management of Heart Failure

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Phenotype congestion, perfusion, ejection fraction, and etiology; do not use EF as a substitute for a cause.
  • For HFrEF, establish the four foundational medication classes early; for HFpEF, prioritize SGLT2 inhibition, decongestion, and treatment of the causal comorbidity cluster.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2021.12.012

2021•Symptoms and diagnostic approach

Chest pain

2021 AHA/ACC Guideline for the Evaluation and Diagnosis of Chest Pain

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use structured risk assessment and high-sensitivity troponin pathways; low-risk patients often need no urgent testing.
  • Select coronary CT angiography, functional testing, or invasive angiography according to age, prior disease, local expertise, and the question being asked.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2021.07.053

2021•Coronary and ischemic disease

Coronary artery revascularization

2021 ACC/AHA/SCAI Guideline for Coronary Artery Revascularization

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Base PCI versus CABG on anatomy, diabetes, ventricular function, operative risk, durability, patient preference, and Heart Team review.
  • Use revascularization urgently for ACS when indicated and for stable disease when symptoms persist or anatomy confers a proven prognostic benefit.

DOI: 10.1016/j.jacc.2021.09.006

2026•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Cardiac rehabilitation

2026 ESC Guidelines on Cardiac Rehabilitation

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Enroll eligible patients after ACS, revascularization, and heart-failure or other qualifying events; referral alone is insufficient without active support for participation.
  • Combine supervised exercise with risk-factor treatment, medication adherence, nutrition, smoking cessation, psychosocial care, and a durable home plan.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag099

2026•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney disease

2026 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Cardiovascular Disease and Chronic Kidney Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Estimate cardiovascular and kidney risk together and protect both organs through blood-pressure, lipid, diabetes, and albuminuria management.
  • Use therapies with outcome benefit—especially SGLT2 inhibitors and renin–angiotensin system blockade when indicated—while monitoring kidney function and potassium.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag098

2026•Coronary and ischemic disease

Universal definition of myocardial infarction

Fifth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Myocardial injury is troponin above the assay’s 99th percentile; infarction additionally requires clinical evidence of acute ischemia.
  • Classify acute versus chronic injury from serial change and identify the mechanism—atherothrombosis, supply–demand imbalance, procedure, or nonischemic injury—before assigning treatment.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehag101

2025•Special populations

Cardiovascular disease and pregnancy

2025 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Cardiovascular Disease and Pregnancy

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Perform preconception risk assessment when possible and use a pregnancy heart team for moderate- or high-risk disease.
  • Balance maternal and fetal effects when choosing imaging, anticoagulation, heart-failure therapy, timing and mode of delivery, and postpartum surveillance.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf193

2025•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Dyslipidaemias

2025 Focused Update of the 2019 ESC/EAS Guidelines for the Management of Dyslipidaemias

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Estimate total cardiovascular risk and pursue lower LDL-C targets as risk rises, with very-high-risk patients receiving the most intensive reduction.
  • Statins remain first line; combine early with ezetimibe and then a PCSK9-directed therapy when the target is not reached.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf190

2025•Inflammatory and pericardial disease

Myocarditis and pericarditis

2025 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Myocarditis and Pericarditis

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use presentation, biomarkers, ECG, echocardiography, and CMR to define pericardial, myocardial, or overlapping inflammation and to identify high-risk features.
  • Restrict exercise during active myocardial inflammation; reserve biopsy and immunosuppression for selected phenotypes in which histology changes management.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf192

2025•Structural and congenital disease

Valvular heart disease

2025 ESC/EACTS Guidelines for the Management of Valvular Heart Disease

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Integrate valve anatomy, lesion severity, symptoms, ventricular response, pulmonary pressure, and trajectory rather than relying on one Doppler number.
  • Refer severe disease before irreversible ventricular damage; choose surgical versus transcatheter intervention through anatomy, age, durability, risk, and Heart Team review.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehaf194

2024•Coronary and ischemic disease

Chronic coronary syndromes

2024 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Chronic Coronary Syndromes

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Choose anatomic or functional testing from pretest likelihood, patient characteristics, local expertise, and the clinical decision at stake.
  • Treat symptoms and event risk in parallel; use invasive angiography and revascularization for high-risk anatomy, refractory symptoms, or strongly abnormal noninvasive findings.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae177

2024•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Elevated blood pressure and hypertension

2024 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Elevated Blood Pressure and Hypertension

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Treat elevated pressure as a continuum of cardiovascular risk and confirm the phenotype with home or ambulatory monitoring.
  • Target systolic pressure intensively when tolerated, while individualizing for frailty, orthostasis, comorbidity, and adverse effects.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae178

2024•Aortic and peripheral vascular disease

Peripheral arterial and aortic diseases

2024 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Peripheral Arterial and Aortic Diseases

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Treat peripheral and aortic disease as manifestations of systemic vascular risk requiring smoking treatment, lipid lowering, blood-pressure control, exercise, and antithrombotic therapy when indicated.
  • Use anatomy, symptoms, growth, embolic risk, and limb viability to determine surveillance and the timing of endovascular or surgical intervention.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehae179

2023•Heart failure and cardiomyopathy

Cardiomyopathies

2023 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Cardiomyopathies

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Begin with phenotype, then pursue etiology using family history, CMR, genetics, laboratory testing, and selected biopsy or nuclear imaging.
  • Treat heart failure, arrhythmia, obstruction, thrombosis, and sudden-death risk while screening relatives when inherited disease is plausible.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehad194

2023•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Cardiovascular disease in diabetes

2023 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Cardiovascular Disease in Patients With Diabetes

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Assess cardiovascular, kidney, and heart-failure risk—not glycemia alone—and intensively treat blood pressure, lipids, smoking, weight, and activity.
  • Use SGLT2 inhibitors and/or GLP-1 receptor agonists with proven outcome benefit according to ASCVD, heart failure, kidney disease, and patient priorities.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehad192

2023•Structural and congenital disease

Infective endocarditis

2023 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Endocarditis

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Obtain multiple blood-culture sets before antibiotics when the patient is stable and use echocardiography plus multimodality imaging when suspicion remains high.
  • Coordinate prolonged targeted antimicrobial therapy, source control, embolic and neurologic assessment, and early surgery for heart failure, uncontrolled infection, or prevention of embolism in selected cases.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehad193

2022•Special populations

Cardio-oncology

2022 ESC Guidelines on Cardio-Oncology

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Estimate baseline cardiovascular risk before potentially cardiotoxic therapy and tailor surveillance to the drug, dose, risk factors, biomarkers, and imaging.
  • Avoid unnecessary interruption of effective cancer therapy: manage toxicity through a multidisciplinary team and use cardioprotective treatment when indicated.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehac244

2022•Perioperative care

Noncardiac surgery

2022 ESC Guidelines on Cardiovascular Assessment and Management of Patients Undergoing Noncardiac Surgery

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Use a stepwise estimate of patient and procedural risk; avoid routine stress testing in low-risk patients or those with adequate functional capacity.
  • Plan perioperative medication and antithrombotic management collaboratively, with postoperative troponin surveillance in selected high-risk patients.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehac270

2022•Pulmonary vascular disease

Pulmonary hypertension

2022 ESC/ERS Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Pulmonary Hypertension

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Confirm pulmonary hypertension with right-heart catheterization after structured clinical, echocardiographic, pulmonary, thromboembolic, and left-heart evaluation.
  • Classify the hemodynamic group before therapy; pulmonary arterial hypertension drugs can harm patients whose mechanism is left-heart or hypoxic lung disease.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehac237

2022•Arrhythmias and devices

Ventricular arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death

2022 ESC Guidelines for the Management of Patients With Ventricular Arrhythmias and the Prevention of Sudden Cardiac Death

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Search for reversible triggers and define structural, ischemic, inflammatory, and genetic substrate with ECG, imaging, monitoring, and selected electrophysiology or genetic testing.
  • Use ICDs for appropriate secondary and primary prevention; combine drugs, ablation, revascularization, and disease-specific treatment according to mechanism.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehac262

2021•Arrhythmias and devices

Cardiac pacing and cardiac resynchronization therapy

2021 ESC Guidelines on Cardiac Pacing and Cardiac Resynchronization Therapy

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Establish symptom–bradycardia correlation and the site of conduction disease; pacing mode should preserve physiologic activation when feasible.
  • Use CRT in appropriately selected patients with systolic dysfunction and electrical dyssynchrony, and reassess response, lead performance, and pacing burden.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehab364

2021•Prevention and cardiometabolic health

Cardiovascular disease prevention

2021 ESC Guidelines on Cardiovascular Disease Prevention in Clinical Practice

Open guideline ↗

Practice-changing essentials

  • Estimate lifetime and near-term risk, then address smoking, activity, diet, adiposity, blood pressure, lipids, diabetes, sleep, and psychosocial factors.
  • Use shared decisions for drug prevention; treatment intensity should reflect absolute benefit, comorbidity, frailty, and patient preference.

DOI: 10.1093/eurheartj/ehab484
